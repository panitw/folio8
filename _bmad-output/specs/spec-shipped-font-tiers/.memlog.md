topic: Retier folio-go's shipped font set so the 10 MB CJK face is opt-in, before the v1.0.0 API freeze
updated: 2026-09-16T21:15
---

- (direction) Split out of SPEC-client-libraries at owner decision: the font-set change gets its own spec because fonts.Shipped() is engine API touching Go callers, the designer and the golden corpus, not just the client libraries.
- (note) MEASURED weights of folio-go/fonts (14 MB total): notosanssc 10 MB (71%), notosans x4 cuts 2.6 MB (19%), roboto x4 cuts 1.5 MB (11%), notosansthai x2 cuts 124 KB (0.9%).
- (note) The owner's opening proposal - drop the Noto family since Roboto is the default - does not survive measurement. Roboto is the default FACE in a chain whose FALLBACKS are Noto, and it covers Latin/Greek/Cyrillic only: no Thai, no CJK. fonts.go records that the starter template names its default chain 'Roboto' over all three families.
- (decision) TIER THE SET, do not drop faces. Core stays embedded: Roboto (4 cuts), Noto Sans (4 cuts), Noto Sans Thai (2 cuts) = ~4.2 MB. Noto Sans SC (10 MB) becomes opt-in. Embedded weight falls 71% and nothing that renders today stops rendering.
- (decision) Noto Sans Thai STAYS in core at 124 KB. Dropping it would save 0.9% and forfeit a first-class concern - the Thai bill-payment barcode fixture, the thai_words.trie dictionary embedded in the engine, and Thai line-breaking.
- (decision) Noto Sans Latin STAYS in core despite being largely redundant with Roboto, because fonts.go keeps the three original Noto names for every document that already names them, body chains from before Story 16.8 included. Removing it is a back-compat break for authored templates.
- (constraint) This must land BEFORE folio-go/v1.0.0 is cut. fonts.Shipped() is public API; under the v1.0.0 semver commitment the same change afterwards is breaking and needs a /v2 import path every caller edits.
- (constraint) Byte-identity is not negotiable here: the golden corpus must render identically after the retiering for every fixture that does not use CJK, and identically with the opt-in package added for those that do. A moved hash is a defect until proven intended.
- (constraint) folio-go/fonts/fonts.go carries a starter_template_test.go that intersects the face names the starter template declares with the Shipped() keys, so the starter and the core tier must stay consistent or that test reds.
- (capability) CAP-1 A caller who renders no Chinese gets a materially smaller dependency without changing any template or call site.
- (capability) CAP-2 A caller who does render Chinese adds one explicit dependency and gets byte-identical output to today.
- (capability) CAP-3 A document naming a face that is not present fails with a diagnostic naming the missing face and the package that provides it, rather than rendering wrong.
- (capability) CAP-4 The designer continues to offer every face it offers today, unaffected by the packaging split.
- (question) What is the opt-in package called, and is it a Go module path (folio-go/fonts/cjk), a separate FontSet constructor (fonts.ShippedCJK()), or both composed by the caller?
- (question) Does the designer keep loading Noto Sans SC as it does today, or does it also become a fetch-on-demand catalogue face like the Story 8.4d/8.5 faces?
- (question) Do any golden corpus fixtures render CJK? If so, the corpus job needs the opt-in package present, and that dependency must be explicit in CI rather than incidental.
- (event) Self-validate pass 1 (coherence) PASS. 4 capabilities, each with intent and success; intents state WHAT (smaller dependency, same output, honest failure, unchanged designer) with the packaging HOW in weight-analysis.md. 7 constraints, each ruling something out - the pre-tag deadline rules out deferral, byte-identity rules out re-subsetting, the starter-template test rules out dropping a starter face. 5 non-goals. Success signal is decided by install size plus corpus hashes.
- (event) Self-validate pass 2 (preservation) PASS. All measured figures preserved. The pre-tag sequencing constraint inherited from SPEC-client-libraries is stated here as a first-class constraint rather than assumed.
- (event) DEFERRED by owner decision. The work is postponed, not rejected, and not superseded: the measurements and the redirected-proposal reasoning in weight-analysis.md stand as written.
- (decision) When this is picked up AFTER folio-go/v1.0.0, the route is ADDITIVE and semver-safe: add a fonts/cjk sub-package and a fonts.ShippedCore(), leaving Shipped() unchanged. The pre-tag deadline constraint in SPEC.md is therefore obsolete and the spec is re-derived without it.
- (note) Second route that survives the deferral: the client libraries build their own FontSet and need not call Shipped(), so binding-side tiering captures the same install saving with no engine change at all. Declined for now.
- (event) Self-validate PASS on the deferred re-derive. Status banner added, the obsolete pre-tag deadline replaced by the additive route in Why and in the constraint it governed, CAP-1 reworded from a default-install claim to an opt-in one, and a new open question added asking whether engine-side retiering is the right route at all now that binding-side tiering is known to be free.
- (note) MEASURED coverage of the shipped NotoSansSC-Regular.ttf (Adobe/Source Han derived, v2.004, 10.1 MB): 31036 glyphs, 30890 mapped codepoints. CJK Unified 20976/20992 (99.9%), CJK Ext-A 6582/6592 (99.8%), Katakana 100%, Hiragana 96.9%, CJK punctuation 100%, Bopomofo 89.6%, Basic Latin and Latin-1 100%, Fullwidth forms 93.3%. Partial and irrelevant here because Roboto and Noto Sans already cover them: Latin Ext-A 23%, Greek 34%, Cyrillic 26%. CJK Ext-B essentially absent (54 of 42720).
- (note) WHY THE FACE IS 10 MB: the two CJK ideograph blocks are about 27500 of its 31036 glyphs. It is not a font with a large alphabet, it is 27500 ideographs, so the weight cannot be trimmed by subsetting without deciding which ideographs to lose - which is why the tier boundary is the whole face.
- (decision) The tier is renamed in substance: it is a CHINESE AND JAPANESE tier, not a CJK tier. Measured, Japanese depends on this same face - kana and kanji are all present - so calling it CJK understates who the opt-in package is for and would let a Japanese caller conclude it is not for them.
- (constraint) Japanese and Traditional Chinese RENDER through this face but are not typographically styled for it: the glyph shapes are Simplified Chinese regional variants, so characters that differ by region (matching the well-known cases such as the Japanese forms of U+76F4 and U+9AA8) come out with mainland conventions. Codepoint coverage is complete; regional correctness is not. Any doc page naming this tier must say so rather than implying Japanese support is typographically correct.
- (note) KOREAN IS NOT COVERED AT ALL, by this face or any other in the shipped set: Hangul Jamo 0/256 and Hangul Syllables 0/11172, so Korean text renders as tofu. Measured, not inferred. This is a PRE-EXISTING gap in folio8's shipped set, not one the tiering creates - recorded here because this is where the coverage evidence now lives.
- (question) Korean renders as tofu today with no diagnostic. Should a missing-coverage condition raise a diagnostic of its own - distinct from CAP-3's missing-FACE diagnostic, since the face IS present and simply has no glyph - or does that belong in a separate spec about coverage reporting?
